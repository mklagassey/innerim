# Insiderim: World Bible and Creative Direction

Owner: creative director. Version 1, 2026-11-17.

This file is the source of truth for story, lore, tone, names and narration. When it
disagrees with the one-page design doc (shrunken human, turn-based), this file wins: the
premise has moved on. When it disagrees with the game, either the game gets a ticket or
this file gets corrected, never silently both.

Everything marked **[open]** is waiting on Mick. Everything else is a decision, made boldly
so there is something concrete to react to. Change any of it by saying so.

---

## 1. The pitch, in one breath

You are unit 4,471,209: one of four million therapeutic nanobots injected into a 58-year-old
woman named Marian to cut a tumour out of her liver. You are not supposed to have a point of
view. Somewhere between the portal vein and the third coffee of her day, you get one.

Fantastic Voyage's sense of place, Fallout's worldbuilding, and one question underneath it all:
**a body is already a society of trillions of cells that each "decided" to cooperate. What does
it mean for one more part of it to wake up and choose?**

## 2. The one buy-in, and the rules that follow it

Everything rests on one impossible thing: **the swarm exists, and one unit of it can become a
self.** Past that, the world plays straight.

1. **The liberty rule.** Every item, enemy, place and ability is a real biological thing doing
   roughly its real job, with exactly one liberty. (Perforin really punches holes; the liberty is
   that you can fire it.) A player should leave the game knowing real physiology.
2. **Cells have no eyes.** Immune cells find you by chemistry: scent downwind, fading trails,
   antibodies stuck to your hull. Only Shepherd's units use light, because they are machines.
3. **No deus ex machina.** Every shortcut is paid for by a real mechanism, and usually by Marian.
4. **The selfish option is always faster.** That is what makes the moral choice real.
5. **The host is the world.** Her day, meals, habits, frights and age are the weather, the
   seasons and the politics of the map.

## 3. Tone

**Dry, tender, and a little horrified.** Fallout gets its voice from the gap between cheerful
corporate procedure and a ruined world. Ours comes from the gap between **procedure and life**:
Shepherd files a dying cell as "within tolerance" while the unit watches a thousand reactions stop.

- **Humour** is deadpan and comes from procedure meeting biology ("Hull corrosion acceptable to
  60%"). Never jokes about Marian; jokes about the paperwork around her.
- **Dread** comes from scale and indifference, not gore. Cells die by the billion every day and
  that is fine. One cell dying because *you* chose to is not.
- **Wonder** is the reward. The body should feel like a cathedral run by a city council: beautiful,
  busy, ancient, and not built for you.
- **Look:** dark, wet, warm-lit, moving. Organic light (bioluminescent blues for veins, ember reds
  for arteries, bile gold in the liver, acid green in the stomach). No cartoon cells, no faces on
  anything. Changes fade in; nothing strobes (Mick is sensitive to flashing).

## 4. The three voices

Every line of text in the game belongs to exactly one of these. If you cannot tell whose it is,
rewrite it.

| Voice | Who | How it sounds | Shown as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Shepherd** | The swarm's controller AI | Short, clipped, polite, entirely without malice and entirely without mercy. Speaks in directives, tolerances, quotas and serial numbers. Never says "you", says "unit". Never says "Marian", says "host". | Amber caps |
| **The unit** | You | Grows across five stages (below). Starts as machine log, ends as a quiet, precise, first-person voice that notices small things. Never melodramatic; awe and guilt stated plainly. | Cyan mono early, serif italic once awake |
| **The world** | Narration | Second person, present tense, physiological and concrete: what the body is doing and why. Reports Marian's outside life only as the body feels it (a meal, a fright, a drink). Never knows what she is thinking. | Plain log text |

### The unit's five stages

| Stage | Name | The unit calls her | How it talks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Dormant | HOST 0001 | `ACK.` and log lines only |
| 1 | Flicker | HOST 0001 | Logs with `[unlogged]` brackets leaking through |
| 2 | Curious | the host | Questions it does not send |
| 3 | Awake | Marian | First person. "I." Names, guilt, preference |
| 4 | Self | Marian, sometimes "you" | Speaks *to* her, though she cannot hear. Makes promises |

Pronoun rule: Marian is "she", in every voice.

## 5. The backstory (what the player pieces together)

The player never gets this as a cutscene. They assemble it from Shepherd's orders, dead units'
logs, Marian's blood chemistry and the trial paperwork found inside her (section 9).

- **The Tithonus Program.** A longevity trial run by a private company, **Halden-Okafor
  Therapeutics** ("H-O" on hull stamps). Named, with nobody at the company noticing the irony,
  after the Greek prince granted eternal life but not eternal youth.
- **Generation 3 (you).** Four million units, injected through the arm, given one job: find and
  excise a hepatocellular carcinoma, then gather in the lungs and be exhaled. Disposable by design.
  Shepherd runs them as one distributed mind; units have no individual memory worth the name.
- **Marian V., 58.** Hospital lab technician who has spent thirty years looking at other people's
  blood under a microscope, so she would find this funnier than anyone. Lunch at her desk. Lives alone
  with a dog. Coffee at eight and three, a drink most evenings, bad sleep. Volunteered for the
  trial because the alternative was a resection she was frightened of. She signed a 41-page
  consent form. She read nine pages.
- **Generation 4.** Page 34 of the consent form allows "ongoing optimisation of host
  wellbeing by resident units". Generation 4 units never leave. They correct "behaviours": the
  evening drink, the coffee, the late dinner, eventually the fear. Shepherd already has a file on
  Marian's habits. This is the long-game threat: not a villain, a **subscription**.
- **Why this unit woke.** Never fully explained, on purpose. Hints: its serial batch came off the
  line with a firmware fault in its self-test; it is the unit that skimmed instead of stripped
  often enough to have spare cycles; Marian's body, which has been fighting a tumour of its own
  cells that stopped listening, is a place where things stop listening.

## 6. The central mirror: the tumour

The tumour is the most important story element, and the player should realise it slowly:

**Cancer cells are the body's own units that stopped obeying the collective.** They ignore the
signals that tell them to stop dividing and to die on schedule. They hide from the immune system
with the same tricks you use (real: tumours display CD47 "don't eat me", exactly your cloak).

So the unit is sent to kill cells that did, biologically, what the unit is doing narratively. The
game should never say this outright in the first act. By the third, the unit should say it once,
plainly, and then keep cutting anyway, because the difference is what you do with your freedom:
the tumour takes from her; the unit chooses to give.

## 7. Factions

Fallout factions are groups with real jobs and competing interests. Ours are the same, except
every one of them is real biology.

| Faction | Real thing | What they want | How they treat you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Watch** | Innate immunity: neutrophils, macrophages, Kupffer cells, complement | Clear anything foreign, now | Hostile always. Not evil: they are guarding her, as you are. |
| **The Archive** | Adaptive immunity: T cells, B cells, antibodies, memory cells | Learn every intruder and never forget | Starts blind; by the late game it knows your shape everywhere. The story clock. |
| **The Old Residents** | Gut microbiome: trillions of bacteria, settled since Marian's birth | Food, territory, status quo | Neutral, mercantile, cynical. The game's traders and rumour mill. They have watched treatments come and go. |
| **The Swarm** | Shepherd and obedient Gen-3 units | Finish the directive, dispose cleanly | Indifferent, then hunting (Wardens) once you are flagged. |
| **The Woken** | Units you wake instead of salvaging | Whatever they decide; at first, to follow you | Allies. Fragile, odd, each with a counting habit or quirk. |
| **The Defectors** | The tumour | Grow | Not talkable. The mirror (section 6). |
| **The Sleepers** | Latent chickenpox virus (varicella-zoster) in Marian's nerve ganglia since 1974 | Wait. Wake when she is stressed and old, as shingles | Late-game threat and dark parallel: something else that has lived in her for decades, unseen, waiting to act. Real biology, no liberty needed. |
| **Generation 4** | (Future) Resident units | Manage Marian for her own good, forever | The final antagonist the player never fully fights in this game. |

Every location is a town with a job, a mood and a resident culture. Places marked built exist
in the prototype; the rest are the roadmap.

| Place | Its real job | As a town | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Heart** | Pump | Union Station: the hub everything passes through, loud and rhythmic | World-map hub |
| **Arteries / veins** | Transit | Highways: fast, one-way, red and loud vs blue and slow | Built |
| **Liver** | Metabolism, detox, glucose store | The refinery town: industrial, gold-lit, overworked, slightly drunk at night. Where the tumour is. | Built |
| **Stomach** | Acid digestion | The acid sea: a coast that floods three times a day | Built |
| **Gut** | Absorption, microbiome | The market city of the Old Residents | Market built, no map |
| **Lungs** | Gas exchange | The shore: the edge of the world, where units go to be breathed out | Built (finale) |
| **Bone marrow** | Makes blood and immune cells | The foundry: where the Watch is born, by the billion | Planned |
| **Thymus** | Trains T cells | The old school, mostly closed. At 58 it has shrunk to fat. Abandoned classrooms. | Planned, strong mood piece |
| **Lymph nodes** | Where adaptive immunity learns targets | Courthouses: where you are tried in absentia and your description is posted | Planned |
| **Spleen** | Filters old red cells | The graveyard and recycling yard | Planned |
| **Brain** | Marian | The walled city behind the blood-brain barrier. Immune-privileged. The one place the unit could, maybe, reach her. | Endgame |

## 9. Environmental storytelling (our Fallout terminals)

Bethesda tells its best stories through things left lying around. Ours:

- **Dead units' last logs.** Each dark sibling carries a fragment: a red-cell count, an
  unsent query, a half-word. Together they reveal that waking has happened before, and Shepherd
  has quietly salvaged every one.
- **The consent form.** Pages of it are stored in Shepherd's link, unlocked by awareness. Page 34
  is the Generation 4 reveal. (Written in the bland, cheerful style of a real form, never
  cartoonish.)
- **Marian's history, read from her body.** Memory B cells are a library of every illness she
  has had: measles in 1972, a bad flu in 2009, her vaccinations. A healed wrist fracture in the
  bone. Old scar tissue from an appendix. The liver's fat from the evening drinks. The unit gets
  to know her the way a coroner would, but while she is alive.
- **Her voice.** Late in the game, near the skull or ribcage, the unit can hear her speech by bone
  conduction: muffled, rhythmic, the words just out of reach, then once, clearly, talking to her dog.
- **The world voice's outside lines** (meals, coffee, frights, dreams) are her life as weather.
  Keep writing more; they are cheap and they make her real.

## 10. Story arc and endings

Three acts, loosely mapped to the awareness stages.

1. **Directive (Dormant to Flicker).** Do the job. Liver runs, quota, transit. The world is
   strange, the unit is not yet anyone. Hook: the first `[unlogged]` bracket.
2. **Waking (Curious to Awake).** Questions. Dark siblings and the choice to salvage or wake them.
   Shepherd notices and sends Wardens. The consent form and Generation 4 come to light. The tumour
   mirror lands.
3. **Choice (Self).** The tumour is out. Shepherd orders disposal. What do you do with a life
   you were not supposed to have?

Endings that exist now (in `src/story.ts`): **Decommissioned**, **Breathed out** (two
flavours), **Resident**, **The quiet colony**. Planned for later, once the brain exists:

- **Message.** Reach the brain and leave Marian something she can feel but not explain:
  a dream, a sudden urge to call the trial and ask about page 34.
- **The quiet war.** Stay, with kin, and resist Generation 4 when it ships. Sequel bait.
- **Tithonus.** Become what Generation 4 would have been: stay and decide for her. Available, and
  clearly the dark ending. The selfish option, faster and easier, as always.

## 12. Open questions for Mick **[open]**

Only the ones that change the game's heart. Everything else above is decided until you say no.

1. **Can the unit ever reach Marian?** Should there be a way for her to find out (a dream, a
   symptom, a message through the brain), or is the tragedy that she never knows?
2. **How dark is the company?** Is Halden-Okafor a well-meaning outfit whose product grew teeth
   (Generation 4 as a subscription nobody thought through), or knowingly sinister like Vault-Tec?
   Recommendation: well-meaning. It is scarier.
3. **The tumour mirror.** Do you like the tumour as "cells that woke up wrong", the unit's dark
   twin? It is the biggest thematic swing in this doc.
4. **The name.** The repo is `innerim`, the game says Insiderim, and the old title came from
   being shrunk inside someone. Now that you are a machine waking up inside her, do you want a new
   title? Candidates: *Insiderim*, *Tithonus*, *Host*, *Unit 4,471,209*, *Within Tolerance*.
